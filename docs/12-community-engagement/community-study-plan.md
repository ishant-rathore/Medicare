# Medicare — Community Study Plan

## Purpose

This plan defines how Medicare's Community Engagement Project (CEP) field study should be carried out and documented. The study must use actual community evidence and must not fabricate participant information or findings.

## 1. CEP Objectives

- Understand practical medication-reminder difficulties faced by senior citizens.
- Identify accessibility, language, reminder and navigation needs.
- Validate the senior-first/voice-first design direction.
- Gather evidence to improve the prototype.
- Prepare a controlled short community deployment/trial.

## 3. Method

### Before Visit

- Prepare interview questionnaire.
- Prepare consent form.
- Prepare a non-sensitive demonstration build.
- Define what evidence will be recorded.
- Avoid collecting diagnoses, prescription details or unnecessary personal information.

### During Visit/Interview

Ask practical questions about:

- number/timing of medicines;
- frequency of forgotten or delayed doses;
- preference for text, sound or spoken reminders;
- regional-language needs;
- readability of normal smartphone notifications;
- difficult controls/navigation;
- usefulness of family-recorded voice;
- preferred handling when a reminder is not acknowledged.

### After Visit

- Summarize observations without identifying participants.
- Map observed needs to product/design requirements.
- Record changes made because of actual evidence.

## 4. Ethics and Privacy

- Participation must be voluntary.
- Obtain appropriate consent before interviews or trials.
- Do not collect unnecessary medical information.
- Use anonymous/aggregated reporting where practical.
- Keep participant evidence secure.
- Do not publish private voice recordings/photos without authorization.

## 5. Prototype Evaluation Tasks

A participant may be asked to:

1. Open Medicare.
2. Review today's medicine list.
3. Understand a reminder.
4. Complete Taken/Snooze/Skip.
5. Adjust voice/accessibility settings.
6. Use the app without network connectivity.
7. Explain whether the reminder was understandable and easy to act on.

## 6. Measurements

Use only measurements that are actually collected, for example:

- task completion;
- assistance required;
- reminder acknowledgement behavior;
- participant feedback;
- observed accessibility issues;
- offline reminder behavior.

Do not invent target achievements as actual outcomes.
